---
title: Speeches and the GSL
---

The General Speakers List (GSL) is committee's default state — whenever nothing else has been
moved into (a [[caucusing|caucus]], a vote, anything else), debate runs through the GSL.

## How the list works

Delegates raise their placard to be added, and the chair records them in the order raised. Some
conferences let you join the list at any point; others "close" it periodically so it isn't
infinite. Either way, once you're on it, you'll get called in turn.

Each speech has a fixed time limit the committee sets (commonly somewhere around 60–90 seconds),
and the chair enforces it — usually with a time warning as you approach the limit.

## Yielding your remaining time

If you finish before your time is up, you don't just sit down — you yield the remainder, and how
you yield is itself a small decision:

- **Yield to the Chair** — the simplest option. You give up the rest of your time and the chair
  moves to the next speaker. This is the default most delegates use.
- **Yield to another delegate** — hand your remaining time to a specific delegate you name. That
  delegate generally can't yield it onward again to a third person.
- **Yield to questions** (also called "yield to comments" at some conferences) — open the floor
  for other delegates to question you on what you just said. Time only counts down while you're
  answering, not while a question is being asked. Not every conference allows this option, so
  check before you plan a speech around it.

Yielding to questions is a genuinely useful tool if you want your position tested and sharpened in
front of the room — but it also means you need to actually be ready to defend what you said.
