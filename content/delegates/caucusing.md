title: Caucusing
---

[[motions|Motions]] cover how a caucus gets started; this is about what actually happens once
you're in one.

## Moderated caucus, in practice

Debate stays formal, just faster and more focused than the GSL. Delegates remain seated; the
chair calls on them one at a time, usually by raised placard, to give short remarks on the
sub-topic named in the motion. It's still chair-controlled: you're not free to just start talking,
and the clock only runs while someone is actually speaking.

## Unmoderated caucus, in practice

This is the one that surprises new delegates most: for however long the motion specifies,
delegates leave their seats entirely and talk freely, with no chair-mediated turns at all. It is
not a break. It's consistently described as where the substantive work of committee actually
happens: finding delegations with aligned positions, merging smaller groups into blocs, and
drafting the [[working-papers-and-resolutions|working paper]] that will eventually become a
resolution.

## Lobbying

"Lobbying" is the informal negotiating that fills unmoderated time: walking the room, finding
allies, building support for a working paper before it has enough backers to move forward. It's
not a separate procedure so much as a name for what unmoderated caucus time is actually for.

If you're new to committee, the practical advice most guides converge on is the same: don't spend
your first unmoderated caucus watching from your seat. This is the part of committee where
relationships and blocs form, and it happens fast.
