---
title: Voting procedure
---

Once debate closes, committee moves into voting. There are two different kinds, and they don't
follow the same rules.

## Procedural votes

Things like setting the agenda or moving into a caucus. **No abstentions** — every delegate votes
yes or no. These generally pass by simple majority.

## Substantive votes

Adopting an [[amendments|amendment]], adopting the resolution itself. **Abstentions are allowed**
here, and — mirroring real UN practice — abstentions aren't counted in the denominator when
figuring out whether a majority was reached. If you answered "present and voting" at
[[committee-basics|roll call]], you've given up the option to abstain on these.

## Simple vs. two-thirds majority

- **Simple majority**: more yes-votes than no-votes among those actually voting.
- **Two-thirds majority**: required for some substantive actions, and echoes how the real General
  Assembly treats "important questions" (peace and security, budget matters, admitting or
  expelling members).

Which threshold applies to adopting a resolution itself is genuinely one of the most
conference-variable numbers in all of MUN procedure — some treat two-thirds as the standard for
any resolution, others reserve it only for specific "important" topics the way the real UN does,
defaulting to simple majority otherwise. Don't assume either without checking.

## Roll call votes

Any delegate can usually request one, and most rule sets require the chair to grant it.
Delegations are called alphabetically (from a randomly chosen starting point) and state their
vote aloud. Beyond plain Yes/No/Abstain, some conferences also allow:

- **Yes/No with rights** — explaining your vote, sometimes used when a delegate votes against
  their country's real-world position and wants to note why.
- **Pass** — defer your vote to the end of the list, though you'll have to vote yes or no (no
  abstaining) when your turn comes back around.

Real UN cross-check: the actual General Assembly defaults to an unrecorded show-of-hands vote —
a roll call only happens if specifically requested — and majorities there are calculated only from
members "present and voting," the same abstention-excluded logic MUN inherited.
